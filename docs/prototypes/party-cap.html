Party Cap Lab

How far apart in skill may a party be in the one rated queue? ADR 0018 fixed the cap's shape: shown in tiers and divisions, enforced on whichever is wider, rank or hidden rating, wider for bigger parties, and a full 5-stack may be any width for fewer points. This sheet sets the numbers. Each run is one simulated day of ADR 0012's queue (built on the queue-structure sheet), now with each party member's own skill, rank and rating.

What's new here. Friends' skills are correlated: each member's skill is √ρ of a shared draw plus √(1−ρ) of their own. Rank lags rating: each player's shown rank sits a random number of divisions off their rating. The cap reads the wider of the two spreads. A party over the cap drops a member (ADR 0018's "the fix is always plain"): the member furthest out queues solo, until the rest fit the cap for their new size. The settings also offer splitting everyone up, or turning the party away. Carry: ADR 0010 rates a team as the sum of its players plus a party bonus, so a party's spread on its own changes nothing. If a strong player lifts weaker friends (each plays some share of the way up toward the best), a wide party is stronger than its sum. The matchmaker can't see that; it only knows the party bonus it learned, which here is the real average over all parties queuing. Sessions end: after each match a player logs off with chance 1 in 4 (default) and a new one arrives, so a day has a few hundred parties, not a few dozen.

A fix to the queue-structure sheet. It only let a party log off when the region would stay at or above its curve without all of them, which almost never fit, so parties piled up: its "25% of players in parties" ran at about 48% of matched players, and ADR 0012's "68% close" was measured at about half the players in parties. Fixed here, 25% in parties gives about 83% close at 200 (no cap, full lobbies).

Words used on this page
Queue
One pool of players the matchmaker can put in a match together. Ranked and casual are separate queues only if the structure splits them; each mode picked separately is another queue.
Rating
A player's hidden skill estimate. Every structure here matches on it; only rated matches move a player's rank.
Rank
The tier, division and points a player is shown (ADR 0011). It moves only in rated matches.
Party
Players who queue together and are put on the same team.
Spread
How far apart a party's highest and lowest members are, in divisions (3 per tier). The cap reads the wider of the rank spread and the hidden-rating spread.
Blocked
A party wider than its cap when it tries to queue. Here it drops its furthest-out member (who queues solo) until it fits, unless the settings say otherwise.
Carry
How much a party's best player lifts the rest: each weaker member plays that share of the way up toward the best. 0 is ADR 0010's model, where only the sum counts.
Out of depth
A player whose own skill is more than 25 percentile points from the average of the lobby they were put in.
Close (real)
The favourite's real chance, carry included, is at most 55%. "As predicted" uses what the matchmaker knows.
Bot
An AI stand-in for a missing player, shown as a bot. A match with a bot in it is never rated.
Team size
The most players a team can have. Every team starts with one player.
Starting player
A team's first player. With “once every team has a starting player”, a match can begin with only these.
Joined player
A player who comes in when the team opens a player recipe. Here, a player in the queue can take that seat in a match already being played.
Skill band
How far apart in skill the matchmaker lets players be. It starts narrow and doubles at a set interval while a player waits.
Peak and off-peak
The 4 busiest and the 4 quietest hours of the simulated day, by players online.
