Rating Lab

Which rating system should run matchmaking and the ladder, and how should it score parties and team results? This page simulates a small game: about 200 players whose real skill is known to the simulation but hidden from the rating systems. They queue into team matches, win or lose, and each rating system tries to work out who is good. Every system plays its own copy of the same population, so you can compare how fast each one learns, how accurate it ends up, what parties do to it, and how it places new players.

Assumptions: a match is won by the team whose players' skill plus luck adds up higher, the same model TrueSkill assumes, which slightly favours the Gaussian systems. Team sizes 2v2 to 4v4 come from the issue, and 5v5 is added because ADR 0006 picked it. A player counts as settled once their ladder position stays within 15 places in 100 of where their real skill belongs, for the rest of the run.

What the numbers mean
Ladder accuracy
Rank correlation between rating and real skill across all players. 0.95 means the order is nearly right; 0.8 means many players are in the wrong band.
Settled by
Median (and 90th percentile) match after which a player's ladder spot stays within 15 places in 100 of the right one. It reads optimistic early in a run, when there is little run left to prove a player stays put.
Odds error
Average gap between the system's predicted win chance and the true one. Lower is better. It is what the matchmaker uses to build fair matches.
Fair matches
Share of matches where the true win chance was between 40% and 60%.
Underdog matches
Share of matches where the system gave one side under 40%.
Rating drift
How far the population's average rating has moved from the start. Above 0 means inflation.
Stat farmers
Players who chase knockouts and crates over winning. Their figure is how many ladder places in 100 they sit above where they belong.
Party bonus
How much stronger a party plays than its players' skills add up to, per extra member, in skill points (TrueSkill 2 found this is real and grows with party size).
